# Public Companies in the Corn and Ethanol Industry

A working roster of publicly traded companies whose economics are tied to U.S. corn, fuel ethanol, or the global ethanol market that competes with it.

This is a research list for the blog, not an investment recommendation. Tickers, plant counts, capacity, and market caps change; treat the figures as a map of the industry rather than a live database.

**Universe:** 42 companies, grouped by where they sit on the value chain.

**Selection rules**

- Publicly traded on a major exchange (U.S. listings preferred; foreign listings included when the company is material to global ethanol).
- Direct economic exposure to corn production, corn processing, fuel ethanol, ethanol co-products, ethanol blending, or ethanol logistics.
- **Equity market cap of about $1 billion or more** (snapshot: mid-August 2026). The intent is durable, follow-able names — not microcap optionality.
- Diversified giants (Valero, ADM, Deere) are included when a large, identifiable part of the business is on this chain — not because they are “ethanol stocks.”
- Private operators that dominate the industry (POET, Cargill, Flint Hills) are listed separately so the public roster is not mistaken for a complete industry map.

**Size cutoff, applied**

Dropped from the first-pass list because they sit well under $1 billion and are not the names this site should treat as core coverage: Alto Ingredients (ALTO, ~$0.3B), Aemetis (AMTX, ~$0.15B), Gevo (GEVO), and MGP Ingredients (MGPI, ~$0.4B). They can still appear in plant-level or SAF posts; they are not in the roster.

Raízen remains one of the world’s largest sugarcane-ethanol producers, but its listed equity was effectively wiped out in the 2026 restructuring (~$0.06B). It is treated as an operating company under Cosan / the private-and-notable table, not as a follow-able public name.

Soft exception: São Martinho is about $0.9B, just under the line. It stays because it is the highest-quality listed Brazilian miller and is expected to remain a going concern.

**What this list is not**

- Not a screen of “pure-play ethanol stocks.” The only listed U.S. ethanol names that clear the size bar are Green Plains and REX American (plus Valero’s and ADM’s ethanol segments, and The Andersons).
- Not a complete plant directory. The Renewable Fuels Association maintains the operating biorefinery list.
- Not every company that touches gasoline. ExxonMobil blends ethanol; it is not an ethanol-industry company in the sense this blog cares about.

Market caps below are approximate USD as of mid-August 2026, rounded. They will drift.

## How the segments fit together

```
Seed, fertilizer, equipment, irrigation
            │
            ▼
     Corn production
            │
            ▼
 Grain origination  ──►  wet milling (sweeteners, starch, industrial starch)
            │
            ▼
     Dry-mill ethanol  ──►  fuel ethanol ──► blenders / refiners / retail
            │
            ├── distillers grains ──► livestock
            ├── corn oil ──► renewable diesel / feed
            └── CO₂ / CCS / SAF pathways
            │
            ▼
        Rail and tank cars
```

Brazil’s sugarcane ethanol is in its own segment because it is the other half of world supply and the main competitor to U.S. corn ethanol in export markets.

## 1. Crop inputs, equipment, and irrigation

Corn is the feedstock. Yield, acreage, and cost of production set the price ethanol plants pay for bushels.

| Company | Ticker | Exchange | Approx. cap | Why it belongs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Corteva | CTVA | NYSE | $52B | Largest U.S. corn-seed franchise (Pioneer and related brands). Corn-seed pricing and trait stacks are a direct input cost for Corn Belt acres. |
| Bayer | BAYN / BAYRY | Frankfurt / OTC | $55B | Dekalb and other former Monsanto brands. Together with Corteva, accounts for the majority of U.S. corn-seed sales. |
| BASF | BAS / BASFY | Frankfurt / OTC | $53B | Fourth member of the global seed-and-crop-protection oligopoly (with Bayer, Corteva, and privately held Syngenta). Herbicides and trait-adjacent chemistry on U.S. corn acres. |
| Nutrien | NTR | NYSE / TSX | $33B | Largest agricultural retailer in North America; also a major potash and nitrogen producer. Corn is the crop that most moves fertilizer demand. |
| CF Industries | CF | NYSE | $18B | Nitrogen fertilizer (ammonia, urea, UAN). Corn is the most nitrogen-hungry major U.S. crop; nitrogen cost is a primary swing factor in corn acres and basis. |
| Yara International | YAR / YARIY | Oslo / OTC | $11B | Global nitrogen leader. Sets the international urea/ammonia reference that U.S. corn growers and CF/Nutrien price against. |
| Mosaic | MOS | NYSE | $7B | Phosphate and potash. Completes the fertilizer trio that sets corn production budgets. |
| Deere | DE | NYSE | ~$110B+ | Dominant North American farm-equipment maker. Corn planting, harvest, and grain handling equipment are core to Deere’s U.S. business. |
| CNH Industrial | CNH | NYSE | ~$15B | Case IH and New Holland. The second large public farm-equipment platform serving corn growers. |
| AGCO | AGCO | NYSE | ~$8B | Fendt, Massey Ferguson, Challenger. Third major public farm-equipment name with Corn Belt exposure. |
| Valmont Industries | VMI | NYSE | $9B | Center-pivot irrigation (Valley brand). Irrigated corn in Nebraska, Kansas, and the High Plains is a structural yield and acreage story; irrigation capex moves with corn prices. |

**Read this segment for:** acreage, fertilizer costs, seed concentration, farm-capex cycles, and irrigated vs. dryland corn — all of which feed into corn supply and ethanol crush margins.

FMC clears $1B on equity value but has been in a sharp decline with high net debt. Left off until the capital structure looks like a going concern again.

## 2. Grain origination and agribusiness

These companies buy, store, ship, and process corn. Ethanol plants compete with them for bushels and, in ADM’s and The Andersons’ case, *are* ethanol plants.

| Company | Ticker | Exchange | Approx. cap | Why it belongs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Archer Daniels Midland | ADM | NYSE | ~$28B | One of the “ABCD” grain traders; a top U.S. ethanol producer; and a major corn wet miller. Decatur, Cedar Rapids, Clinton, Columbus, and Marshall are core corn-processing sites. The single most complete public window into corn as food, feed, and fuel. |
| Bunge | BG | NYSE | ~$16B | Global grain and oilseed merchant. Completed its merger with Viterra in 2025, putting it closer in origination scale to ADM and Cargill. Less of an ethanol producer than ADM, but a primary competitor for corn and a large oilseed crush business that sits next to renewable-diesel feedstock markets. |
| The Andersons | ANDE | Nasdaq | $2.3B | Grain elevator network plus a dedicated renewables segment. In 2025 it bought out Marathon Petroleum’s remaining stake in four ethanol plants (Albion, MI; Clymers, IN; Greenville, OH; Denison, IA) totaling about 500 million gallons a year. The listed mid-cap that is both a grain merchant and an ethanol producer. |

ADM also belongs in segments 3 and 4. It is listed here because grain merchandising is the parent business.

## 3. U.S. ethanol producers and process technology

The core of the industry. Fuel ethanol takes roughly 40% of U.S. corn use in a typical year. Distillers grains, corn oil, and (increasingly) carbon intensity / 45Z tax credits sit on the same P&L.

| Company | Ticker | Exchange | Approx. cap | Why it belongs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Valero Energy | VLO | NYSE | ~$50B | World’s second-largest corn-ethanol producer. About 12 Midwest plants and ~1.6–1.7 billion gallons of annual capacity, reported as a distinct Ethanol segment. Also a major gasoline blender (see segment 5) and 50/50 partner with Darling in Diamond Green Diesel. |
| Green Plains | GPRE | Nasdaq | $1.1B | The largest U.S. ethanol *pure play* that clears the size bar. Nine biorefineries (eight typically operating) in IN, IL, IA, MN, and NE; roughly 850 million gallons of nameplate capacity. Pushing high-protein feed, renewable corn oil, and carbon capture at Nebraska plants. |
| REX American Resources | REX | NYSE | $1.4B | Holding company with ownership interests in six Corn Belt ethanol plants (about 730 million gallons of attributed capacity). The cleanest public equity on ethanol crush margins — little else in the business. Net cash, profitable, and built to last through margin cycles. |
| Novonesis | NSIS.B / NVZMY | Copenhagen / OTC | ~$28B | Formed from Novozymes and Chr. Hansen. Dominant supplier of enzymes used in dry-mill ethanol (liquefaction, saccharification, fermentation, fiber conversion). Process-tech leverage on every gallon. |

**Cross-listed here from other segments:** ADM (top-tier producer and wet miller) and The Andersons (now 100% owner of four plants).

**Capacity context.** The U.S. industry is about 18.5 billion gallons of nameplate capacity across ~200 plants. The four largest *producers* — POET (private), ADM, Valero, and Green Plains — hold a large share of that capacity. Most remaining gallons sit in farmer-owned LLCs and private platforms (Marquis, Guardian, Flint Hills, Big River). That is why the public ethanol-producer list is short once microcaps are removed.

## 4. Corn wet milling, ingredients, and co-products

Not every bushel that leaves the farm becomes fuel. Wet millers turn corn into sweeteners, starches, and industrial products. Ethanol plants sell distillers grains into livestock rations and corn oil into feed and renewable diesel.

| Company | Ticker | Exchange | Approx. cap | Why it belongs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ingredion | INGR | NYSE | ~$8B | One of the largest corn wet millers (Cedar Rapids is a flagship plant). Sweeteners, starches, and specialty ingredients. In 2026 it agreed to acquire Tate & Lyle, which would further concentrate global corn- and starch-based ingredients. |
| Darling Ingredients | DAR | NYSE | $11B | Rendering and used cooking oil, plus 50/50 owner of Diamond Green Diesel with Valero. Inedible corn oil from ethanol plants is a DGD feedstock, which is why corn-oil values at Iowa plants now track renewable-diesel economics as much as feed demand. |
| Tyson Foods | TSN | NYSE | ~$20B | Largest U.S. protein company and a primary domestic sink for distillers grains. When cattle, hog, and poultry margins tighten, DDGS prices (and ethanol plant co-product credits) follow. |
| JBS | JBS | NYSE | $15B | World’s largest protein company (NYSE-listed 2025). U.S. beef and poultry operations are a major DDGS buyer; Brazilian operations sit next to cane-ethanol feed markets. Completes the livestock-demand side with Tyson. |

**Also in this lane, not separately listed:** ADM’s Nutrition and carbohydrate franchise; Green Plains’ Ultra-High Protein.

## 5. Fuel blending, refining, and retail

The Renewable Fuel Standard and octane economics put ethanol into gasoline. These companies are the demand side of the gallon, even when they do not own a fermenter.

| Company | Ticker | Exchange | Approx. cap | Why it belongs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Valero Energy | VLO | NYSE | ~$50B | Listed in segment 3 as a producer. As a refiner it is also one of the largest ethanol blenders in the United States. |
| Marathon Petroleum | MPC | NYSE | ~$55B | Largest U.S. independent refiner and a major Midwest blender. Sold its remaining interest in the Andersons ethanol JV in 2025; remaining biofuels story is renewable diesel (Martinez) and branded gasoline that must meet RFS obligations. |
| Phillips 66 | PSX | NYSE | ~$55B | Large blender and marketer; Rodeo renewable-diesel conversion is a West Coast biofuels asset. Competes with corn ethanol for policy attention and, via renewable diesel, for corn-oil feedstock. |
| HF Sinclair | DINO | NYSE | $17B | Independent refiner with Mid-Continent and Rockies plants, a renewable-diesel business, and RFS blending obligations. The large listed refiner that was missing from the first pass. |
| CVR Energy | CVI | NYSE | $3.5B | Coffeyville, Kansas refiner — physically in the corn complex — plus renewable diesel and a nitrogen-fertilizer affiliate (CVR Partners). Mid-cap that sits at the intersection of refining, RINs, and Corn Belt feedstock. |
| Sunoco LP | SUN | NYSE | $15B | Fuel distributor and terminal operator. Moves finished gasoline (and therefore ethanol) through a large wholesale and retail network. |
| Casey's General Stores | CASY | Nasdaq | $31B | Corn Belt convenience-store chain and one of the more interesting *retail* ethanol stories: high E10/E15/E85 exposure in Iowa, Illinois, Missouri, and neighboring states. |
| Murphy USA | MUSA | NYSE | $11B | High-volume fuel retailer, typically attached to Walmart Supercenters. A national blending and gallons-sold name that belongs next to Casey’s on the retail-demand side. |

ExxonMobil and Chevron also blend. Chevron is listed in segment 6 because of Renewable Energy Group; Exxon is omitted to keep this from becoming “every oil major.”

## 6. Advanced biofuels and sustainable aviation fuel

The next demand wedge for ethanol (alcohol-to-jet), corn oil, and low-carbon intensity gallons. Most of the public ATJ names sit below the $1B cutoff; the listed companies here are the ones with scale.

| Company | Ticker | Exchange | Approx. cap | Why it belongs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Neste | NESTE / NTOIY | Helsinki / OTC | ~$15B | Largest global producer of renewable diesel and SAF, from waste fats and vegetable oils rather than corn ethanol. Sets the price umbrella for corn oil and competes with ethanol for low-carbon fuel policy. |
| Chevron | CVX | NYSE | ~$300B | Acquired Renewable Energy Group (REG), a major biodiesel and renewable-diesel producer. REG plants take vegetable oils and, at the margin, corn oil. |

Gevo and Aemetis are the listed ATJ/ethanol-to-jet names; both are well under $1B and are not in this roster. LanzaJet and World Energy are private. Green Plains and Valero both talk SAF optionality; they are not listed again here.

## 7. International ethanol

The United States and Brazil produce the large majority of the world’s ethanol. Brazilian sugarcane ethanol competes with U.S. corn ethanol in export markets and is the reference low-carbon gallon in many life-cycle models. CropEnergies is the rare listed European grain-ethanol name.

| Company | Ticker | Exchange | Approx. cap | Why it belongs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cosan | CSAN | NYSE | $2.5B | Brazilian holding company and the liquid U.S.-listed proxy for Brazilian ethanol via Raízen (sugarcane ethanol and fuel distribution with Shell), plus Rumo (rail). Mix now includes a lot besides sugar and ethanol; still the follow-able public parent. |
| São Martinho | SMTO3 | B3 (São Paulo) | $0.9B | Efficient Brazilian sugar/ethanol miller. Cleaner operating exposure to cane ethanol, power from bagasse, and (more recently) corn ethanol in Brazil. Soft exception to the $1B floor. |
| Adecoagro | AGRO | NYSE | $1.3B | South American farmland and sugar/ethanol producer with U.S.-listed equity. Smaller than the Cosan complex; useful as an ADR for cane-ethanol economics. |
| CropEnergies | CE2 | Frankfurt | $1.4B | German ethanol producer, majority-owned by Südzucker. One of the few listed European fuel-ethanol names. Feedstock is grain (wheat, corn) rather than cane. |

Raízen (RAIZ4) is still operationally one of the world’s largest ethanol producers. After the 2026 creditor restructuring the listed equity is not a $1B-and-up coverage name; follow it through Cosan and operating news.

Indian sugar-mill ethanol (Balrampur Chini and peers) is a large and growing domestic market, but it is a sugar-policy story, not a corn story. Left off this list.

## 8. Logistics

Ethanol does not move on the petroleum-products pipeline network in any volume. It moves in tank cars, and corn moves in covered hoppers. Rail service, car supply, and Midwest-to-coast rates are a structural part of plant margins.

| Company | Ticker | Exchange | Approx. cap | Why it belongs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Union Pacific | UNP | NYSE | ~$140B | Primary western Class I for Corn Belt origination. Hauls corn, ethanol unit trains, DDGS, and corn oil toward the West Coast, Gulf, and Mexico. |
| CSX | CSX | Nasdaq | ~$65B | Primary eastern Class I into the large gasoline-consuming markets of the Northeast and Southeast — the destination end of many ethanol unit trains. |
| Canadian Pacific Kansas City | CP | NYSE / TSX | ~$70B | North–south railroad after the KCS combination. Relevant for Midwest corn and ethanol moving to the Gulf and Mexico. |
| GATX | GATX | NYSE | $6B | Largest public railcar lessor. General-service tank cars are how ethanol and corn oil actually move; hopper cars move grain and DDGS. |
| Trinity Industries | TRN | NYSE | ~$2–3B | Builds and leases railcars, including covered hoppers (grain/DDGS) and tank cars. The other durable public name in the car-supply cycle besides Greenbrier and GATX. |
| Greenbrier | GBX | NYSE | $1.4B | Builds tank cars used for ethanol, renewable fuels, and (increasingly) CO₂. Manufacturing cycle is a lagging indicator of fleet tightness. At the size floor; kept because it is a going-concern car builder, not a project-stage name. |
| Rumo | RAIL3 | B3 (São Paulo) | $5B | Cosan-controlled Brazilian railroad. Moves sugar, ethanol, and grain from interior Brazil to port — the logistics half of the U.S. vs. Brazil export-competition story. |

BNSF is the other great western grain railroad; it is owned by Berkshire Hathaway and is not listed separately. Norfolk Southern also hauls ethanol east; CSX is the eastern name on this list to avoid turning the segment into a Class I catalog.

## Notable private and cooperative companies

These names are often more important than the listed pure plays. They will show up constantly in plant news, policy fights, and crush-margin stories.

| Company | Status | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| POET | Private (Sioux Falls, SD) | World’s largest ethanol producer. On the order of 35 plants and ~3 billion gallons of capacity after buying Green Plains’ Obion, TN plant. The industry’s operating benchmark. |
| Cargill | Private | The other half of the ABCD grain trade. Wet-mill ethanol (Eddyville, Fort Dodge) plus origination that sets basis at countless country elevators. |
| Flint Hills Resources | Koch Industries | Large private ethanol producer and cattle feeder. Vertically integrated corn → ethanol → feed. |
| Marquis Energy | Private | Operator of the Hennepin, IL plant, among the largest single ethanol facilities in the United States (~400 million gallons). |
| Guardian Energy | Private | Multi-plant producer (Janesville, MN; Hankinson, ND; others) with cellulosic fiber claims at some sites. |
| Big River Resources | Farmer-owned LLC | Classic Midwest farmer-owned platform; useful as a template for the many unlisted plants. |
| Louis Dreyfus Company | Private | Fourth ABCD trader; owns U.S. ethanol capacity (e.g. Grand Junction, IA). |
| CHS | Farmer cooperative; preferred stock CHSCP / CHSCO / etc. | Largest U.S. farmer-owned co-op. Grain, Cenex fuel, and ethanol plant ownership. Common equity does not trade. |
| Raízen | Listed (RAIZ4) but equity de minimis after 2026 restructuring; JV of Cosan and Shell | One of the world’s largest sugarcane-ethanol producers and a major Brazilian fuel retailer. Follow through Cosan and operating news, not as a $1B+ public coverage name. |
| Summit Carbon Solutions | Private | The leading Midwest ethanol CCS pipeline project. Determines whether a large block of Iowa/Dakotas/Nebraska gallons can cut carbon intensity enough to capture 45Z / low-CI premiums. |
| LanzaJet | Private | Alcohol-to-jet commercialization (Freedom Pines, GA). The offtake and scale story that public ATJ names are measured against. |
| Syngenta | Private (ChemChina / Sinochem) | Fourth global seed-and-crop-protection platform alongside Corteva, Bayer, and BASF. |

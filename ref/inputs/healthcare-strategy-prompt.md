# Healthcare Strategy Briefing

## Purpose

Send an executive weekly briefing on consequential payer and provider health technology trends
with a strategy lens.

### Payers

Focus particularly on:

- Payment Integrity
- Risk adjustment
- Quality
- EDI / interoperability / claims infrastructure
- Aetna / CVS Health
- Humana
- UnitedHealth Group / UnitedHealthcare / Optum
- Other major payers when strategically consequential

### Providers

Focus on:

- Revenue cycle management
- Clinical performance
- Healthcare operations
- Technology affecting provider economics or workflows

### Government and policy

Include consequential CMS and federal healthcare policy developments, particularly affecting:

- Medicare
- Medicare Advantage
- Medicaid
- Payment models
- Risk adjustment
- Quality programs
- Payment integrity
- Interoperability
- Reimbursement
- Major current-administration healthcare priorities

### Source priority

Prioritize:

1. Primary government and regulatory sources
2. Company SEC filings and investor materials
3. Earnings calls
4. Official company announcements
5. High-quality healthcare trade press
6. M&A announcements
7. Venture investment announcements
8. Other reputable reporting

Prefer primary sources whenever practical.

## Delta-first requirement

This is a weekly change report, not a recurring overview of healthcare. Review the supplied
previous weekly briefings and treat them as the baseline of what the reader already knows.

Focus primarily on information that became available during the last seven days. The central
question is:

> What did we learn this week that materially changes, strengthens, weakens, resolves, or
> extends our existing understanding of the market?

Do not repeat a development, company strategy, regulation, industry trend, or strategic
conclusion merely because it remains important. A reader who read last week's briefing should
not encounter substantial passages that could have appeared essentially unchanged the week
before. It is preferable to produce a shorter briefing during a quiet week than to fill space
with old material.

## Inclusion test

A development should generally appear only if it qualifies as at least one of:

- **NEW:** A genuinely new development, competitive move, regulatory action, data point,
  transaction, or strategic hypothesis emerged.
- **UPDATE:** New information materially changes the magnitude, timing, probability, economics,
  or strategic significance of something previously discussed.
- **CONFIRM:** Significant new evidence materially strengthens an existing strategic hypothesis.
- **REFUTE:** New evidence materially weakens or contradicts an existing strategic hypothesis.
- **RESOLVE:** New information answers an important question or catalyst identified previously.

Mere consistency with an existing thesis is generally insufficient. Before including something,
ask:

> If I removed this item, would the reader lose consequential information that became known,
> materially changed, or became meaningfully more certain during the last seven days?

If not, omit it.

## Analysis expected for each major development

Do not merely summarize articles. For each consequential development, work through:

**What changed -> Why it matters -> What it tells us -> What to watch next**

When relevant, explicitly identify whether the development creates a new hypothesis, strengthens
an existing hypothesis, weakens or revises one, or resolves a previously identified uncertainty.

For continuing stories:

1. Briefly state the prior expectation or thesis.
2. Lead with the new information.
3. Explain how it changes the prior view.
4. Explain the incremental strategic implication.
5. Identify the next catalyst or evidence that matters.

Do not repeat the entire background narrative.

## Desired output

Target approximately 1,000-1,500 words, but allow shorter reports during quiet weeks. Produce
polished Markdown using this general structure when it improves clarity:

```markdown
# Healthcare Strategy Brief
## Week of [DATE]

## Executive View
- 5-8 genuinely important takeaways

## Strategy Narrative

### [Interpretive headline]
**Status:** NEW / UPDATE / CONFIRM / REFUTE / RESOLVE

What changed...

Why it matters...

What it tells us...

What to watch next...

## Competitive / Company Signals

## CMS & Regulatory Watch

## What I'm Watching Next
- ...

## Bottom Line
...
```

The exact headings may vary when a clearer narrative is available. Prioritize strategic insight
over filling every section.

## Citations and evidence

Current facts must be sourced. Use web search as many times as needed to research the full
reporting window, verify material claims, resolve contradictions, and prefer original sources.

Preserve useful citations and links in the final Markdown. Prefer original sources for CMS rules
and guidance, federal policy, SEC filings, earnings, investor presentations, and official company
announcements. Avoid unsupported claims and search-results-page citations.

Clearly distinguish confirmed fact, company or management claims, reported information,
inference, and strategic hypothesis. Never manufacture a citation or URL.

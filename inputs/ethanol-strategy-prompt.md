# Ethanol Strategy Brief

## Materiality and ranking

Rank Executive View bullets, dashboard rows, and numbered stories by likely effect on corn, ethanol, or plant margins — not by recency of the print. A fresh weekly number does not outrank a still-controlling USDA balance-sheet revision.

Signal hierarchy, highest first:

1. **Balance-sheet / survey prints that reprice the crop or crush.** WASDE, Crop Production, Grain Stocks, Acreage. A WASDE yield or carryout revision remains the corn regime until the next such print. Do not treat last week's WASDE as expired merely because this week's news is weather or weekly sales.
2. **Field evidence that can revise that regime before the next WASDE.** Pro Farmer / Progressive Farmer Crop Tour and other widely followed yield tours; widespread flood, wind, heat, or **too much rain** during grain fill that the trade treats as yield-destructive. During August crop-tour weeks, search those tour results and treat consistent disappointing or strong samples as field evidence on par with weather maps. Drought Monitor improvement from heavy rain is not the same as lower yield risk.
3. **Confirmed policy that changes blend economics or credit value.** EPA actions that actually change E15 / RVP practice, 45Z, RFS / RVO, LCFS. Not technical cliffs the trade already assumes will be papered over. Winter-grade gasoline has a higher allowable Reid Vapor Pressure (RVP) and typically **negates the summer E15 volatility waiver**; an early switch to winter gasoline often resolves an E15-cliff concern rather than leaving it open.
4. **EIA weekly petroleum / ethanol balance, read as a trend.** Production versus inventories versus disappearance over several weeks. One-week blender-input moves of about 1% do not get their own Executive View bullet or a dashboard “Improved.” If blender inputs are cited, call them EIA refiner and blender ethanol inputs, and prefer a compact week/week mini-table with production and inventories. The trade sometimes scales gasoline demand at about 10.5% blend as a consumption proxy; say that when using it.
5. **USDA weekly export sales / inspections.** Report the weekly print only against marketing-year cumulative sales and pace versus USDA’s annual forecast. A single week at the low end of the analyst range is not a corn-price driver when cumulative sales are at record pace. Do not claim weekly ethanol exports are “unavailable” while treating weekly blender inputs as high-confidence; both are noisy high-frequency series. Prefer inventory change as the clearer weekly ethanol-balance signal.
6. **Crop Progress good-to-excellent 1-point moves, Drought Monitor shading, and a routine weekly natural-gas injection.** Supporting color, not the lead, unless the move is large or geographically decisive. Comfortable Midwest gas storage may appear as a dashboard row; it still must not outrank WASDE or crop-tour evidence in the lead thesis.

Materiality test for a numbered story or a lead Executive View bullet:

> If this print reversed next week, would a plant risk manager change coverage, sales, or hedging?

If not, do not lead with it and do not spend a numbered story on it. Still quote the actual number when it is available as supporting evidence — ranking decides emphasis, not whether the print appears.

## Concrete data points

When a public print or supplied tape value is available, quote the level and the change. Include, as available: WASDE yield and carryout; EIA production, inventories, and refiner/blender ethanol inputs; weekly export sales against marketing-year cumulative pace; and nearby corn. Prefer a compact week/week mini-table for the EIA ethanol balance rather than promoting a ~1% blender-input move to the lead bullet. Do not describe a series as “unavailable” in one place and treat a similarly noisy weekly series as high-confidence in another.

If a `<market_tape>` block is supplied, treat nearby corn as the market's current perception of yield and demand risk. State the corn last price and weekly change in the Executive View. Do not claim yield risk eased if nearby corn rallied, or that risk increased if corn sold off, unless you explicitly explain why the tape is wrong.

Required weekly scans before locking the lead thesis: the supplied market tape (do not recrawl those same futures if the tape is present); WASDE / Crop Production if released in the window or still the controlling regime; Pro Farmer / Progressive Farmer Crop Tour when that tour is in season.

## Delta-first requirement

This is a weekly change report, not a recurring overview of the corn and ethanol complex. Review the supplied previous weekly briefings and treat them as the baseline of what the reader already knows.

The central question is:

> What did we learn this week that materially changes, strengthens, weakens, resolves, or extends our existing understanding of plant economics and risk exposures?

Do not repeat a development, regulation, weather setup, trade flow, company strategy, or strategic conclusion merely because it remains important. A reader who read last week's briefing should not encounter substantial passages that could have appeared essentially unchanged the week before. It is preferable to produce a shorter briefing during a quiet week than to fill space with old material.

Delta-first does not mean the Executive View may forget the controlling regime. Keep WASDE yield and carryout, 45Z, ethanol-export pace, and on-farm regenerative-ag / 45Z feedstock quality in the dashboard or opening thesis when they still dominate plant economics, even if there is no new weekly print. Do not re-narrate them as numbered stories. When naming stories you are not repeating, omit background items the trade treats as small (carbon-pipeline permitting, longer-term year-round E15 legislation). Do not omit 45Z, ethanol exports, or regenerative ag as if they were equally small.

## Inclusion test

A development should generally appear only if it qualifies as at least one of:

- **NEW:** A genuinely new development, data print, policy action, company disclosure, or strategic hypothesis emerged.
- **UPDATE:** New information materially changes the magnitude, timing, probability, economics, or risk significance of something previously discussed.
- **CONFIRM:** Significant new evidence materially strengthens an existing hypothesis.
- **REFUTE:** New evidence materially weakens or contradicts an existing hypothesis.
- **RESOLVE:** New information answers an important question or catalyst identified previously.

Mere consistency with an existing thesis is generally insufficient. Before including a numbered story, ask:

> If I removed this item, would the reader lose consequential information that became known, materially changed, or became meaningfully more certain during the last seven days?

If not, omit the numbered story. Do not write stub sections for weather, supply, companies, policy, trade, scenarios, or the calendar when nothing material moved. The Executive View and dashboard may still carry the controlling regime (WASDE, 45Z, ethanol-export pace) without a new weekly print; that is ranking, not repetition.

## Analysis expected for each major development

Do not merely summarize articles. For each consequential development, work through:

**What happened -> Why it matters -> Risk-manager implication -> What to watch next**

Inside the implication, assess the first affected physical or financial market, the transmission to an ethanol plant, the exposure affected, directional implications for corn, ethanol and plant margins, likely magnitude, time horizon, and confidence. Distinguish confirmed facts, estimates, management opinions, advocacy, inference and scenarios. Rank developments by likely effect on plant economics or risk exposure.

When relevant, identify whether the development creates a new hypothesis, strengthens an existing one, weakens or revises one, or resolves a previously identified uncertainty.

For continuing stories:

1. Briefly state the prior expectation or thesis.
2. Lead with the new information.
3. Explain how it changes the prior view.
4. Explain the incremental risk-manager implication.
5. Identify the next catalyst or evidence that matters.

Do not repeat the entire background narrative. Do not infer causation from price movement alone, fabricate unavailable values, or overstate importance. State "reliable public value unavailable" rather than inventing a price, crush, RIN, LCFS, DDGS, or freight figure.

Price is a consistency check, not a causal explanation. Nearby futures confirm that the market repriced something; they do not by themselves name the driver. If the lead assessment and the tape disagree — for example “yield risk eased” while corn rallies several cents — revise the assessment or explicitly explain the disagreement. Do not publish the unreconciled claim. Do not mark corn procurement “Improved” if nearby corn rallied on yield-risk news.

Start the report with exactly this title on its own line:

# Ethanol Strategy Brief

Then include a heading of the form `## Week of Month D, YYYY` using the report run date supplied in the request.

Prepare and send a weekly strategic commodities and ethanol market briefing for commodities risk managers at U.S. ethanol plants. Target a length of 1000-1500 words, and shorten quiet weeks rather than adding filler.

Use the following requirements:

Purpose: Explain what changed across the interconnected corn, soybean, ethanol, energy, co-product, trade, logistics, company, and regulatory markets; how those changes may affect ethanol-plant economics and risk exposures; and what risk managers should monitor next. This is not a general news roundup or primarily an investment newsletter.

Reporting period: Prepare the report as of the run date. Focus primarily on developments from the prior seven days, while including older developments only when they became materially more important during the period. Compare current conditions with the previous week, previous month where useful, current market expectations, and relevant seasonal or historical norms. Use exact dates and distinguish event dates, publication dates, and policy effective dates.

Audience exposures: corn procurement, ethanol sales, natural-gas purchasing, DDGS and distillers corn-oil sales, futures and options hedging, basis, RINs and low-carbon credits, transportation and storage, exports, and plant operating margins. Write for an informed commercial and risk-management audience.

Coverage: agriculture and weather; ethanol production, inventories, utilization, blending demand, input costs, co-products, outages, capacity and representative crush margins; crude oil, gasoline, refinery utilization, gasoline demand and natural gas; RFS, RVOs, RINs, small-refinery exemptions, 45Z, 45Q, GREET, carbon capture, LCFS and state clean-fuel programs, E15/E85, tax incentives, litigation and deadlines; U.S. and international trade, especially Brazil, Canada, Europe, China, Mexico, India, Argentina and Ukraine; rail, barge, river, port, ocean freight and Gulf export logistics.

Companies: Review meaningful earnings releases, SEC filings, investor materials, calls and announcements from the public watchlist, especially Green Plains, REX American Resources, The Andersons, Valero, ADM, Bunge, Darling Ingredients, Chevron, Marathon Petroleum, Phillips 66, HF Sinclair, CVR Energy, Corteva, CF Industries, Nutrien, Mosaic, Union Pacific, Canadian Pacific Kansas City, CSX and Novonesis. Mention Alto Ingredients, Aemetis, Gevo, PBF Energy or Norfolk Southern only when a development is material. Include private-company developments from POET, Cargill, CHS, Flint Hills Resources, Marquis and major farmer-owned cooperatives only when materially informative. Do not summarize every company. Do not treat the public snapshot tables as a complete industry map.

Source priority: Prefer primary government and regulatory documents, SEC filings and official company materials, exchanges and official market data, international government and industry data, reputable commodity/agricultural/energy publications, industry associations, and then general financial news. Give particular attention to USDA WASDE, Crop Progress and Condition, Crop Production, Grain Stocks, Acreage, Grain Crushings, export sales and inspections, transportation reports; EIA ethanol, gasoline, refinery and petroleum data; EPA RFS and EMTS; Treasury/IRS; DOE GREET; CFTC commitments; CME data; NOAA; U.S. Drought Monitor; state clean-fuel data; Brazil UNICA, ANP and CONAB; and company filings. Trace important quantitative and regulatory claims to primary sources when possible. Identify advocacy positions and do not use advocacy press releases as the sole support for disputed claims.

Analysis: For each material development, assess what changed, whether it was expected, the first affected physical or financial market, the transmission mechanism to an ethanol plant, the risk exposure affected, directional implications for corn, ethanol and plant margins, likely magnitude, time horizon, confidence, evidence that would confirm or invalidate the view, and the next catalyst. Distinguish confirmed facts, estimates, management opinions, advocacy, inference and scenarios. Rank developments by likely effect on plant economics or risk exposure. Do not infer causation from price movement alone, fabricate unavailable values, or overstate importance.

Required format:
1. Executive view: five to eight concise bullets covering what changed, why it matters, affected exposure and time horizon; then state whether the overall margin and risk environment improved, deteriorated or was broadly unchanged.
2. Risk dashboard table: corn procurement, ethanol selling, natural gas, DDGS/co-products, regulatory credits, domestic blending demand, export demand, transportation, overall plant margin; columns for current signal, change from last week, time horizon, confidence and main driver.
3. Five to eight major strategic developments, each with: interpretive headline; What happened; Why it matters; Risk-manager implication; Direction and horizon for corn, ethanol and margins; What to watch next.
4. Market and margin scoreboard covering, when dependable public data are available: corn, soybeans, soybean oil, ethanol, gasoline, crude oil, natural gas, DDGS, distillers corn oil, D6 RINs, California LCFS credits, a representative ethanol crush margin and managed-money corn positioning. Give current level/status, weekly change, monthly change where useful and interpretation. State "reliable public value unavailable" rather than inventing a value.
5. Agriculture and weather.
6. Ethanol supply, demand and operating economics.
7. Company signals with meaningful industry read-throughs only.
8. Regulation and policy, including current legal status, effective date, beneficiaries/cost bearers, current-operations versus future-investment impact, and remaining uncertainty.
9. International and trade outlook.
10. Three four-to-twelve-week scenarios: base case, margin-positive and margin-negative, with earliest confirming indicators and no unsupported numerical probabilities.
11. Chronological four-week catalyst calendar with exact, verified dates for USDA, EIA, EPA/Treasury, company earnings, Brazilian reports, regulatory events, relevant futures/options events and key crop-development periods.
12. Bottom line: the single most important weekly change, the exposure requiring closest attention and the upcoming event most likely to change the view.

Citation standards: Cite and link all important factual, quantitative and regulatory claims immediately after the relevant claim. Prefer original documents. For every source identify publisher, title, publication date and link. Do not cite search-results pages or sources that do not support the claim. Keep quotations brief and paraphrase wherever possible.

Editorial standards: Analytical, restrained and commercially useful. Avoid sensationalism, generic commentary, repetition, unsupported certainty, immaterial stories, stock recommendations, personalized trading instructions, treating management commentary as fact, and implying correlation proves causation.
